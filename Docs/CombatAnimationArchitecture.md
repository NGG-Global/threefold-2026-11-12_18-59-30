# Combat animation architecture

Sprint 1 of the combat animation work gives the Mage animated combat: a stance per discipline,
the melee combos (three strikes for Arcane and Flame, two for Aegis), charge start, hold, release
and cancel, block, stagger, aerial attacks and defeat. It also gives effects a reliable set of
timing cues (wind-up, effect, hit window, contact or release, recovery, cancellation).

**Status.** Written and compiled against the Unity 6000.6.0f1 assemblies (Editor and Android
player defines); the rules pass 39 tests outside Unity. **Nothing has been seen playing in Unity or
on a device.** The clips are CC0 placeholders chosen for silhouette and timing, not designed for the
Mage. See [Verified and not verified](#verified-and-not-verified).

## How it works

```
 Input ──► DisciplineCombatModel / MeleeComboMachine (rules, plain C#, own clock)
                │ SwingStarted / SwingLanded / SwingInterrupted / ChargeBegan / ChargeEnded
                │ PlayerGuard.Blocked, Health.Damaged, defeat, climbing (read each frame)
                ▼
        CombatAnimationDirector (MonoBehaviour)
                │ feeds
                ▼
        CombatAnimationModel (plain C#) ──► layer states, speeds, weights ──► Animator
                │                                                    (CrossFadeInFixedTime by state name)
                └──► CombatCueTracker ──► Cue events ──► effects, sound, anything
```

**The rules lead; the animation follows.** The combat rules decide when a hit lands and when the
next action may start. The animation never deals damage, never delays a hit and never extends an
action. This keeps every existing rule, test and number exactly as it was.

### Layers

The setup adds three layers between the locomotion layer and the casting layer:

| Layer | Mask | Shows | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base Layer | none | Locomotion, jump, fall, land, traversal (unchanged) | 1 |
| CombatStanceFull | none | The selected discipline's stance on the whole body, so a low or wide stance shows its legs (added in Sprint 2, `Docs/MartialStyles.md`) | Faded in while in combat and standing, out by 0.6 m/s |
| CombatStance | upper body | The selected discipline's stance | Faded in while in combat and standing, out with speed |
| CombatFullBody | none | Committed attacks while standing still, aerial attacks, defeat | 1 while playing, 0 otherwise |
| CombatUpperBody | upper body | Every attack, charge, block and reaction; full-body slots while moving | 1 while playing, 0 otherwise |
| Cast | upper body (arms) | The earlier casting gesture | 0 while the director is active, 1 otherwise |

The upper-body mask covers the head, arms, fingers and, by default, the spine. **Whether the
spine part of a Humanoid mask also turns the hips is not verified;** if the legs twist or slide
during upper-body attacks while running, untick *Upper Body Includes Torso* on the set and run
*Apply Combat Animation Set*.

The combat layers have no transitions. The director cross-fades to a state by name when the model
asks for it, so no trigger can stay set after an action was cancelled, and returning to locomotion
is a fade of the layer to empty. A one-shot slot has a second state (`_B`) so the same slot can
follow itself with a blend.

### Slots

A **slot** is one combat moment: which clip plays and when, inside that clip, the moments that
matter happen. Gameplay never sees a clip name; the generated states are named after the slots
(`Arcane_Attack2`, `Shared_BlockHold`), and `CombatSlotId` produces the same names for the director.

| Per discipline | Shared |
| --- | --- |
| Stance; one attack slot per melee step; charge start, hold, release, cancel; aerial attack and aerial release (optional) | Block hold, block impact, stagger, heavy stagger, aerial attack, aerial release, defeat |

Each slot carries: the clip name, mirror, scope (upper or full body), the striking hand or foot,
and moments in **normalised clip time** (entry, hit window open, contact, hit window close, effect,
recovery end), plus blend times and the clip's length. The setup fills in the length from the model.

Fallbacks, so a missing animation never stops an action (`CombatSlotResolver`):

- In the air: the discipline's aerial slot, then the shared aerial slot, then the grounded slot on
  the upper body.
- A melee step beyond the attack slots uses the last one.
- A full-body slot plays on the whole body only on the ground below 0.6 m/s, or in the air for an
  aerial slot. Otherwise it plays on the upper body, so feet never slide.
- An unassigned slot plays nothing; its cues are still sent from the rules' timing.

### Fitting a clip to the combat clock

`CombatTimeWarp` plays the clip's wind-up (entry to contact) at the speed that shows the contact
frame exactly when the rules resolve the hit, and its recovery (contact to recovery end) at the
speed that ends it when the rules allow the next action. Speeds stay between 0.5x and 3x. When a
wind-up would need more than 3x, its beginning is skipped instead; the contact still lands on time.
When the gameplay contact arrives, the recovery speed is computed from where the clip really is.

With the default numbers of `DisciplineDefaults` and the default slots:

| Step | Rules: start-up / recovery | Clip | Clip wind-up | Wind-up speed | Starts at | Recovery speed | Visual recovery vs rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arcane: Arcane Jab | 0.07 s / 0.20 s | `Punch_Jab` | 0.200 s | 2.86x | 0% | 2.50x | +0 ms |
| Arcane: Arcane Cut | 0.07 s / 0.20 s | `Punch_Cross` | 0.300 s | 3.00x | 9% | 3.00x | +11 ms |
| Arcane: Arcane Lance | 0.10 s / 0.30 s | `Spell_Simple_Enter` | 0.433 s | 3.00x | 25% | 0.50x | -100 ms (holds the reach) |
| Flame: Ember Swipe | 0.12 s / 0.26 s | `Sword_Attack` | 0.400 s | 3.00x | 3% | 3.00x | +40 ms |
| Flame: Ember Backswing | 0.12 s / 0.26 s | `Sword_Attack`, mirrored | 0.400 s | 3.00x | 3% | 3.00x | +40 ms |
| Flame: Flare | 0.16 s / 0.36 s | `Sword_Regular_B` | 0.267 s | 1.67x | 0% | 0.56x | 0 ms |
| Aegis: Force Palm | 0.18 s / 0.34 s | `Shield_OneShot` | 0.100 s | 0.56x | 0% | 1.18x | 0 ms |
| Aegis: Bulwark Slam | 0.26 s / 0.46 s | `Melee_Hook` | 0.267 s | 1.03x | 0% | 0.50x | -59 ms (holds the low pose) |

Every contact is shown within one frame (33 ms) of the hit; a test checks this. A recovery that
runs on past the rules is blended over by the next action. Arcane and Flame run at the 3x limit:
the strikes will look very fast, because the design numbers are very fast. Whether that reads well
is a judgement for the owner in Unity.

A charged release fires the moment the player lets go (unchanged). Its clip therefore starts at
its release frame; the charge hold before it is the anticipation.

### Cues: animation-to-gameplay synchronisation

`CombatAnimationDirector.Cue` sends a `CombatCue` (kind, action id, discipline, step, hand or foot,
time) at each moment of an attack:

| Cue | Melee | Charged |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Windup` | Swing started | Charge started |
| `Effect` | Two frames before the hit window | When the hand reaches the charge pose |
| `HitWindowOpened` / `HitWindowClosed` | Around the contact | Not sent |
| `Contact` | The rules resolved the hit (from `SwingLanded`) | Not sent |
| `Release` | Not sent | The charged attack fired (from `ChargeEnded`) |
| `Recovered` | The recovery has ended (terminal) | The release has ended (terminal) |
| `Cancelled` | Abandoned before contact (terminal) | Cancelled or broken (terminal) |

Guarantees, each covered by a test in `CombatAnimationTests`:

- Every cue of an action is sent at most once, in time order.
- `Contact` and `Release` come from the rules, never from a timer, and are accepted once; a repeat,
  or one for an older action, is ignored. **No cue deals damage**: the hit is already resolved.
- Cues timed before the contact that have not been sent when it arrives are sent first, in order.
- Every action ends with exactly one terminal cue; nothing of that action is sent after it.
- An opened hit window is always closed, before the terminal cue at the latest.
- A new action ends the previous one with its terminal cue first.

This replaces Animation Events, which the imported clips do not have, cannot be added to without
editing the model, and which keep firing on a clip that is being blended out after a cancellation.

To use the cues (for example a hand trail during the hit window):

```csharp
director.Cue += cue =>
{
    if (cue.Action != CombatActionKind.Melee) return;
    if (cue.Kind == CombatCueKind.HitWindowOpened) StartTrail(cue.Effector);
    if (cue.Kind == CombatCueKind.HitWindowClosed) StopTrail();
};
```

`DisciplineFeedback` keeps spawning its effects from the combat events, as before. It now asks the
director which hand holds a charge, so the Arcane charge glows in the left (casting) hand.

### What each layer shows when several things happen

Upper body, first that applies: nothing while defeated or climbing; block impact, then block hold,
while blocking; a release; an attack; a stagger; a charge cancel; the charge start, then its hold.
Full body: defeat; a full-body release or attack. A one-shot pushed aside by something more
important never resumes half-way; a hold (block, charge) does.

- **Block** (shared) is the forearm guard. A hit the guard stops plays the block impact, not a stagger.
- **Stagger**: an unguarded hit of 4 damage or more plays the stagger, 10 or more the heavy stagger
  (10 is also the damage that breaks a charge). It is presentation only: the player has no
  gameplay stagger, and a stagger never hides a swing the rules are still carrying out.
- **Interruption**: a swing abandoned by BLOCK, climbing or defeat sends `Cancelled` and blends
  out in 0.1 s. A cancelled charge plays the discipline's cancel clip.
- **Aerial**: an attack or release in the air plays the shared leap pose on the whole body; it ends
  on landing so the landing shows.
- **Stance**: shown on the upper body while standing in combat (blocking, charging, or within 4 s
  of the last combat action), faded out as speed rises to 1.5 m/s.
- **Defeat**: the collapse plays on the whole body and holds until revived.

## Retargeting

Every clip in the model is imported as Humanoid (`CharacterModelPostprocessor`), so any Humanoid
rig plays them and mirroring works. To use another animation source:

1. If it shares the Quaternius rig, add its clip names to `COMBAT_ANIMATIONS` in
   `Tools/blender/build_mage.py`, rebuild the model, and check the result with
   `Tools/blender/compare_mage.py` (existing clips must be unchanged).
2. If it is a separate Humanoid file, put it under `Assets/Threefold/Art/Characters/` so it imports
   as Humanoid, and extend `MageCharacterSetup.LoadModelClips` to read it. (Not implemented: no such
   pack exists yet.)
3. Name the clip in the slot, and measure its moments with `analyse_combat_clips.py`.
4. Run *Validate Combat Animations*: it reports a clip that is missing, not Humanoid, does not loop
   where it must, or cannot show its contact on time.

Third-party animation packs must not be committed without checking their licence first. The
Quaternius libraries are CC0.

## Laptop actions

The Android build workflow and every level setup (`Threefold > Setup > ...`) now do the following
on their own; no separate command is needed:

1. Unity re-imports `Mage.fbx` (import settings version 4) with 29 clips.
2. `MageCharacterSetup` creates `Assets/Threefold/Generated/Characters/Mage/MageCombatAnimations.asset`
   and `MageCombatUpperBody.mask`, and rebuilds `MageAnimator.controller` in place with the combat
   layers (controller version 3). The prefab and scenes keep their reference to it.
3. `CombatAnimationSetup` adds the `CombatAnimationDirector` to the Mage.

Then, in the Editor:

- `Threefold > Animation > Validate Combat Animations` must report no warnings.
- Play the Discipline Range and check the [review list](#review-in-unity-and-on-a-device).
- After changing a clip, mirror, scope or held pose in `MageCombatAnimations`, run
  `Threefold > Animation > Apply Combat Animation Set`. Moments, blends and tuning take effect at once.

## Known limits

- All clips are placeholders on a mannequin; the timings are measurements, not animation direction.
- Arcane and Flame strikes play at up to 3x because the design start-ups are 0.07 to 0.16 s.
- Clips with travel (`Melee_Hook` 0.36 m, `Death01` 0.9 m) are baked in place, so the body moves
  away from the character's position during them and returns as they blend out.
- Held poses (Arcane and Aegis stances, Aegis charge hold) are still: no breathing.
- The player has no gameplay stagger or knockdown; the stagger is visual only.
- Unity reports the clip length; the setup copies it into the set. A clip replaced in the model
  without running the setup keeps the old length until the next setup run.
- The model file grew from 4.0 MB to 10.5 MB (stored through Git LFS).

# Emergent spell combinations

Sprint LM-06 (Living Magic, part 6) connects the three disciplines. Combinations are not recipes:
nothing in the code says "Flame then Aegis breaks stone". Each discipline delivers its effects
(heat, arcane charge, force, a brace), each thing in the world or in combat has properties, and a
combination is what the properties do with the effects. A player who has learnt that heat makes
stone brittle can work out that a heated Guardian's stone plates will crack under force, because
the same rule decides both.

It builds on the effects and properties of LM-03 (`Docs/WorldMagic.md`), the chain reactions and
budgets of LM-04 (`Docs/WorldState.md`) and the discovery cues of LM-05 (`Docs/Discovery.md`).

**Status:** the code compiles against the Unity 6000.6.0f1 assemblies, and the rules pass their
tests outside Unity (see Tests). Nothing has been run inside the Unity Editor or on a device. The
combination court and the Cinder Wisp have not been generated or seen, no projectile has been
turned by a real guard, and no armour has cracked on a real Guardian. The look of armour, ward and
deflection is a placeholder and a proposal. Every number below is a first estimate that has never
been played.

## The four combinations

| Combination | Rule | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heat + force = fracture** | While a structure's thermal property is heated or burning, the structure uses its brittleness for the heat; force above the threshold then removes integrity | `CombinationRules.ForceOnStructure`, used by every structure: barriers, doors, a creature's armour |
| **Arcane + mechanism = activation** | Charge builds to a threshold and the mechanism activates. A moving part may be held until it does (`Movable Only When`), after which force moves it | `ArcaneProperty`; the kinetic lock in `MagicalPropertySet.IsKineticLocked` |
| **Force + projectile = deflection** | A designated projectile is turned aside by a force at least as strong as it demands, from anyone not on its owner's side, once. It keeps what it carries | `DeflectionRules` |
| **Stabilise + instability = bracing** | A brace stops what force and weight would bring down, for as long as it lasts | `StabilityProperty`; weight is new (`Load Stress Per Second`) |

A fifth relationship links objects and creatures: **force against footing**. One force at or above
a threshold takes something off its feet. For an unstable structure that threshold is its collapse
force; for a creature it is its new **stagger force**.

### Consistency across contexts

The same rule gives the same result wherever it applies. The tests check this directly:

- A stone barrier and stone armour with the same values lose exactly the same integrity to the
  same heat and force, cold and heated (`E_StoneArmourAndAStoneBarrierFollowTheSameRule`). The
  Guardian's plates are the barrier's stone (brittleness 0.25 cold, 8 heated, 4 per unit of
  force) in thinner plates (threshold 0.5, integrity 60); what looks different is the animation,
  not the relationship.
- Heat from Flame strikes, a Flame Wave, a burning object nearby or turned fiery motes leads to the
  same heated state, and a blow then has the same result (`A_HeatFromAnySourceGivesTheSameFracture`).
- A Wisp's ward is an arcane mechanism like a crystal: its activation is its disruption.

### Order

No rule looks at the order in which spells arrived. Where order matters, it is because of the
object's state at the time:

- Heated stone breaks under force; cold stone barely marks. A cold blow first is not wasted (it
  removes a little), and the stone still breaks once heated.
- A rune-locked slide cannot be pushed until its lock is lit. That order is the object's own:
  its kinetic property names Activated as the condition under which force moves it.
- Effects that do not act on each other (force and charge on the reinforced door, heat and charge
  on a Wisp) give the same result in either order.

## The combination court

Generated by **Threefold > Setup > Discipline Range**, along the right edge of the range floor
(the six examples of LM-03 stand along the left edge). Nothing in it has a script of its own.

| Object | Properties | Combination | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rune-Locked Slide** | Arcane-reactive (timed, 10 s), kinetic (5 m rail, held until Activated) | Arcane, then force | A full Arcane Arrow, or two uncharged ones in quick succession, lights the lock. While lit, a Force Pulse slides the block (2 m uncharged, 4 m full); Aegis strikes are below its minimum force. When the lock goes dark the block stays where it was left; a glide under way stops. More charge while lit restarts the ten seconds. Its position is saved. A gate beyond the far stop opens for good when the block reaches it |
| **Dry Kindling** | Thermal (catches at 20 heat), structural (burns away in about 4 s) | Fire, from anywhere | One fiery mote or two Flame strikes set it alight. While it burns it sends 25 heat a second to everything within 2.5 m, which sets the wooden barricade behind it alight. Force scatters it |
| **Crumbling Walkway** | Stability (gives way under weight) | Aegis guard | It leads up to a 3.8 m ledge, beyond what the Mage can climb. Anyone standing on it adds 2 stress a second and it gives way at 1.5, so nobody gets across unbraced. Each pulse of the Aegis guard adds 1.5 s of brace, up to 8 s (the walkway's brace stacking is Extend). Hold the guard at its foot for about 2.5 s, then walk up; or keep the guard up while walking, which renews the brace on the way. A full Force Pulse (4.5) brings it down when it is not braced. It comes back on a restart |
| **Cinder Wisp** on the ledge | An enemy (below) | Aegis guard against its motes | Its motes carry 45 heat. Turned aside with the Aegis guard, they go where the crosshair points: into the kindling, at the barricade, or back at the Wisp |

The court also has a wooden barricade behind the kindling and an enemy director, so the Wisp is
paced and comes back on a restart.

Alternative approaches stay open where the rules allow them: the slide's lock can be lit by any
arcane charge (a crystal's linked reaction counts the same as an arrow), the kindling by any heat,
the barricade by Flame directly, and the Wisp can simply be fought.

## Combat

Enemies answer the same effects through the same rules (`CreatureEffectModel`). Each kind of
enemy has some capabilities and none has all of them.

| Capability | What it does | Ruins Guardian | Arcane Wisp | Cinder Wisp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heat | Heats, ignites, burns (as before) | 0.6 times | 1.3 times | Immune |
| Push | Force moves it along its navigation surface (as before) | Resists 60% | Full distance | Full distance |
| **Stagger force** | One force at least this strong staggers it, whatever its poise | 3.5 | 1.5 | 1.5 |
| **Armour** | A structure: takes a share of every hit while it holds; broken, the enemy takes more | Stone plates | None | None |
| **Ward** | An arcane mechanism: takes a share of every hit while it holds; arcane charge at its threshold disrupts it for a time | None | Arcane shell | None |

**Armour.** While intact the plates take 20% of a hit, weakened 10%, fractured nothing; once
broken, the Guardian takes 20% more. Breaking them staggers it. Cold, a full Force Pulse removes
4.5 of 60; once the Guardian is heated (a full Flame Wave and one Flame strike), even an uncharged
pulse breaks them. A burn, which has no direction, passes the armour, as it passes a raised guard.

**Ward.** While it holds it takes 15% of a hit. 40 arcane charge (an uncharged Arcane Arrow)
disrupts it for six seconds and staggers the Wisp; it then forms again. A fully charged Arcane
Arrow still defeats a Wisp at Normal difficulty in one hit through the ward.

**Stagger by force.** A structure coming down beside a Guardian (3.5) staggers it; a stone
shattering (2) does not. The stagger immunity of the enemy AI still applies, so armour breaking
during a stagger does not stagger twice and nothing can be stun-locked. A stagger caused by the
world does not tell the enemy where the player stands.

**Reflected projectiles.** A turned mote now belongs to whoever turned it: it passes over the
player and the player's allies, strikes enemies, and what it does is credited to the player (the
damage source) and to the Aegis discipline (for discovery). It keeps its damage, its kind of magic
and its effects, leaves 30% faster, and can be turned only once, so it cannot be bounced back and
forth. Its range starts again but is no longer than before.

**Enemies and the world act on each other.**

- The Guardian's slam carries force 2.5 and its shockwave 3.5, **for objects only**: a shockwave
  brings down an unstable structure, which then staggers a Guardian standing beside it. These do
  not shove the Mage.
- A Cinder Wisp's mote heats whatever it strikes, the Mage included.
- Creatures already received objects' reactions (a Wisp beside a burning barricade heats up).

### Hazards and the player

The Mage now takes effects from the world, by explicit rules (`PlayerEffectReceiver`):

| Effect | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Heat | The Mage warms at 0.6 of the heat sent, ignites at 100 and burns for 2.5 s at 5 damage a second. Standing within reach of a burning barricade sets the Mage alight after about ten seconds |
| Force | A shove of half the force (a collapsing structure: 1.75 m), through the character controller, never through walls, and not while hanging or climbing |
| Charge, bracing | Nothing |

The Mage's own attacks never reach the Mage. A raised guard takes the same share of an effect from
the front as it takes of a hit (80%). A restart puts out a burn caught from the world.

## The Aegis guard

With Aegis selected, holding BLOCK now also sets force against projectiles that strike the guard
from the guarded side (`Guard: Deflect Force`, 1 by default; an enemy mote yields to 0.5). The
other guards have no force and deflect nothing. The protection BLOCK gives is unchanged. A turned
projectile goes where the crosshair points, aimed as the Arcane Arrow is (look down at the
kindling to send a mote into it); if the sender lies within 25 degrees of that line, measured along
the ground, it goes back at the sender, also up on a ledge. Aegis attacks that carry force (strikes
0.6 and 1, the Force Pulse) also turn aside motes in their area, away from the Mage, but not one
behind cover.

A projectile counts as a thing, not a creature: force meant for creatures only never turns one. The
Guardian's strikes carry force for objects, so they could turn a projectile that can still be
turned; with the default values none can, because the Mage's own shots are not designated and a
turned mote cannot be turned again.

## Balance

Measured with the default numbers, by arithmetic (`CombinationTests`), not by play:

| Without combining (armour and ward stay up) | Arcane | Flame | Aegis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Melee time to defeat a Guardian, before LM-06 | 5.6 s | 6.8 s | 7.4 s |
| Same, with its armour intact all fight | 7.1 s (1.26 times) | 8.4 s (1.24 times) | 9.2 s (1.24 times) |
| Same, once its armour is broken | 4.7 s | 5.9 s | 6.2 s |
| Swings to defeat a Wisp, before and with its ward | 4 and 5 | 4 and 4 | 3 and 3 |

- Single-discipline combat stays viable: no standard enemy takes more than about a quarter longer
  with one discipline, and none is immune to any discipline's damage.
- Combining is rewarded: breaking the armour makes the Guardian fight about a third shorter than
  with the armour intact.
- Melee stays the main damage: the Flame Wave and Force Pulse that break the armour deal under 30%
  of a Guardian's health together.
- No combination needs more than two disciplines, and none needs fast switching: every temporary
  state a combination waits on (heated stone, a heated Guardian, a lit lock, a brace, a disrupted
  ward) lasts long enough to switch (instant) and fully charge a Force Pulse (0.9 s).

These numbers change the established balance of LM-02 for the Guardian (about a quarter longer
without combining). Play-testing must confirm or retune them; the values are in the enemy assets.

## What prevents runaway behaviour

| Risk | Prevention |
| --- | --- |
| Infinite loops | A projectile is turned once at most; reaction depth 3 (LM-04); a ward needs new charge to be disrupted again |
| Self-triggering | A turned projectile ignores whoever turned it and their allies; a side never turns its own shots; an object's reaction never reaches the object itself or comes back by another path (same event identity); an attack never strikes its user |
| Stale weight | The count of who stands on an object is kept while the object is switched off or destroyed, so it is right when the object comes back |
| Unbounded physical simulation | Nothing new uses physics bodies. A turned projectile's range is bounded; weight is a number, not a simulation; reactions stay within the budgets of LM-04 |
| Invisible state changes | Every change of armour and ward is announced (`CreatureEffectModel.StateChanged`) and marked by a burst; while it lasts it is shown (plates, cracks, ward shell). A walkway straining under weight has its own condition (Loaded) with a tint and a burst. A deflection is marked where it happens |
| Repeated damage | One attack is one event: a target takes each kind of effect once per event, so a second collider of the same attack cracks no more armour and a duplicate cannot disrupt a ward or ignite twice |

## Set up

1. Pull `main` on the laptop and let Unity import. The Console must show no red errors.
2. Run **Threefold > Setup > Discipline Range**, read the report and save the scene. It generates
   the three court objects and the Cinder Wisp, places the court, bakes the range's navigation
   mesh, and adds the enemy director.
3. Run **Threefold > Setup > Combat Arena** (or **Threefold > Setup > Disciplines** in a scene that
   has the Mage) once, so that existing assets are updated: the Guardian and Wisp definitions get
   stagger force, armour and ward; the Guardian's strikes get their force for objects; the Arcane
   Mote becomes deflectable; the Aegis guard gets its deflect force; the Guardian and Wisp prefabs
   get the parts that show their defences; the Mage gets its hazard rules. Each of these is filled
   once; values tuned by hand are kept.
4. Run **Threefold > Validate > Open Scene** in each scene.
5. Upload the scenes and the changed or new assets in `Assets/Threefold/Generated/WorldMagic`,
   `Generated/Enemies`, `Generated/Disciplines` and `Generated/Navigation` (see
   `Docs/DevelopmentWorkflow.md`).

## Validation

**Threefold > Validate > Magical Objects** (and the scene validation) now also report:

- A moving part locked by the movement itself (Moving, At Travel Limit), or by a condition the
  object can never reach, so nothing ever moves it.
- Weight with a stress limit of 0, so weight can never bring the object down.
- An object that weight brings down but that has no Load Zone, a Load Zone that reports to another
  object, or one whose collider is not a trigger.

## Performance

- **No update per object.** Combinations add no update to any magical object. Weight is counted
  by trigger callbacks; the object is advanced by the one world simulation while it strains. The
  enemy model's ward drains in the enemy's existing update. `CreatureStateVisuals` has no update.
- **Deflection costs nothing in flight.** A guard is asked only when a projectile strikes the
  player. An attack with force looks through the few deflectable projectiles in flight (motes are
  pooled, at most 16 per kind).
- **No allocation per effect.** As in LM-03.

Not measured: none of this has been profiled.

### Changes to earlier systems

- `ProjectileLaunch` carries a deflection designation; `ProjectileSpellDefinition` has explicit
  effects and a deflection.
- `MeleeStrikeDefinition` carries effects (the Guardian's force for objects).
- `GuardEffectProfile` has a deflect force. `DisciplineDefaults.DataVersion` is 2.
- `EnemyDefinition` has a data version; `EnemyArchetypes.DataVersion` is 1.
- `ThirdPersonMotor.Shove` moves the character for a moment, for hazards.
- `StatusKind` gained Deflected, ArmourCracked, ArmourBroken, WardDisrupted, WardRestored and Knocked.
- `PropertyCondition` gained Loaded. `MagicalObject` has `SetOccupants`, `LoadChanged` and a Load Zone.
- `PrototypeSession.Restart` puts out a burn the Mage caught from the world.
- `CombatArenaSetup.EnsureDirector` is shared with the range.

## Known limits

- Not yet compiled by Unity, run in the Editor, or played on a device.
- All numbers are first estimates, and the Guardian's armour lengthens a fight without combining by
  about a quarter. Play-testing must decide whether that is wanted.
- The look of armour (plates and a crack), ward (a pale shell) and deflection (a burst) is a
  placeholder from primitive shapes. A plate's crack shows from Weakened on; it does not deepen
  gradually.
- A Load Zone counts only colliders that physics reports to a trigger: the Mage's character
  controller does; an enemy moved by its navigation agent does not, so enemies never load a walkway.
- Physics may not report a character leaving a trigger when its collider is switched off. The
  object recounts while it is being simulated, so a missed exit is corrected within a step.
- A collapsed walkway stays down until a restart; it has no persistent id.
- The range's navigation mesh is baked from the range's architecture and the ledge only. Pressing
  Bake on its NavMeshSurface collects the architecture alone and leaves the ledge out; run the setup
  instead.
- The Cinder Wisp stands on a small island of navigation mesh on the ledge. It cannot come down; it
  fights from there.
- Enemies cannot turn projectiles, brace or use the disciplines' effects on purpose; the Guardian's
  strikes act on objects only because they land there.
- There is no sound.

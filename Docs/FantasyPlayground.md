# Fantasy Playground: the first environment

An abandoned magical training courtyard at the edge of an ancient forest, built to test the
Mage's movement and combat. The whole level is generated by an Editor command; no object is
placed by hand and no art files are used.

**Status:** the code has passed a syntax check and the repository checks only. It has not been
compiled in Unity and nobody has seen the level. Proportions, colours and lighting were set by
calculation and judgement and will need adjusting once it can be looked at. This sprint is built
on the touch controls (PR #4) and combat (PR #5), which are in the same unverified state.

There is no Sprint 4 in the repository; this work follows directly on Sprint 3.

## Build it

1. Pull the branch on the laptop and let Unity import. The Console must show no red errors.
2. Run **Threefold > Setup > Fantasy Playground**.
3. Press Play.

The command creates the scene `Assets/Scenes/FantasyPlayground.unity`, generates the modules,
lays out the level, sets up lighting, adds the Mage with camera, controls, combat and HUD,
saves the scene, and puts it first in Build Settings so a device build starts there.

Afterwards, upload the new files from the laptop (see `Docs/DevelopmentWorkflow.md`): the scene,
`Assets/Threefold/Generated/Environment`, `Assets/Threefold/Generated/Combat`, and the changed
`ProjectSettings/EditorBuildSettings.asset`.

### Running it again

| Command | What it replaces | What it keeps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fantasy Playground** | The generated environment in the scene (after asking) | The Mage, camera, HUD, touch controls, training dummies, anything added by hand, and all generated assets |
| **Fantasy Playground (Regenerate Assets)** | The above, plus the generated meshes and prefabs (after asking) | Materials and textures, so tuned colours survive |

Only the object marked as the generated environment is ever deleted. Use the second command
after the generating code has changed. To move the training dummies to the layout of a new
version, delete the `TrainingDummies` object first.

## The level

The courtyard is 36 m square. The player starts at the south gate looking north: across the
open ground to the crystal, up the stair to the terrace, and through the great arch to a ruined
spire on the horizon.

| Area | Where | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open ground | Centre | Walking, sprinting and fighting in the open; a few columns to move around |
| Crystal dais | Centre | The focal point. Standing within 4.5 m restores mana quickly, so a fight moves between the centre and the edges |
| Terrace and stair | North | Stairs, height, shooting uphill; the colonnade gives cover |
| Jump course | East | Blocks rising 0.5 m at a time with gaps of about 1.4 m, up to a platform 2.4 m high. A full jump reaches 1.5 m |
| Ruined wall and arches | West | A passage about 3.5 m wide behind the ruins, to exercise the camera in a confined space |
| Hillside | Outside the walls | About 8 m of walkable slope with trees and rocks, then an invisible wall |
| South gate | Behind the starting point | A portal to the Combat Arena (Sprint 8, `Docs/EnemyAI.md`). Hidden if the arena is not in Build Settings |

Seven training dummies stand at ground level, on the terrace, on the high platform, in the west
passage and out on the hillside, so shots are tested near, far, uphill and past cover.

## Art direction, and how it is achieved without art assets

| Aim | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Strong architectural silhouettes | Round arches of separate stones, tapered columns with bases and capitals, a great arch on the terrace that frames a distant spire |
| Naturalistic materials, stylised colour | Faceted low-polygon shapes with flat shading; warm stone against cool shadow and green moss; three small generated textures for stone, paving and ground |
| Ruin and age | Broken and fallen columns, stepped broken walls, a half-collapsed arch, moss on the tops of the masonry, rubble, grass along the walls |
| Mystery | A glowing crystal turning above a rune-marked dais; cool distance fog; forest closing in |
| Atmospheric depth | Linear fog, forested hills, and a ring of mountains 250 to 320 m away that fade into the fog |
| Cinematic light | A low, warm sun with shadows, cool three-colour ambient light, a generated late-afternoon sky, and the project's existing bloom and tone mapping |

## Reusable modules

Each module is a prefab in `Assets/Threefold/Generated/Environment/Prefabs`, with its mesh in
`Meshes`. They can be placed in any scene.

- **Architecture:** Column, ColumnBroken, ColumnFallen, Arch, ArchRuined, Wall, WallBroken, WallLow, Stairs, Dais.
- **Nature:** TreeA, TreeB, TreeC, RockA, RockB, RockC, GrassPatchA, GrassPatchB, MossPatch, Rubble.
- **Background:** MountainA, MountainB, DistantSpire.
- **Special:** ManaCrystal (turns, bobs, glows and carries a light).

Stone blocks of any size (floor, terrace, jump blocks) are generated on demand as meshes.

Twelve materials are shared by everything. Textures hold brightness only and each material
tints them, so a colour is changed in one place.

## How it is built

| File (under `Assets/Threefold/Scripts`) | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `Editor/Environment/ProceduralMeshBuilder.cs` | Builds flat-shaded meshes from boxes, prisms, cones and triangles |
| `Editor/Environment/EnvironmentAssets.cs` | Generates textures, materials, meshes and module prefabs |
| `Editor/Environment/FantasyPlaygroundSetup.cs` | Creates the scene, lays out the level, lighting and atmosphere, and adds the gameplay rig |
| `Runtime/World/ManaFont.cs` | Restores mana near the crystal |
| `Runtime/World/FloatingMotion.cs` | Turns and bobs the crystal |
| `Runtime/World/GeneratedEnvironment.cs` | Marks the generated root so only it is rebuilt |

The layout uses fixed random seeds, so every rebuild gives the same level.

`MagePrototypeSetup.EnsureCharacterRig` was split out of the Mage setup command. Any future
level command can call it to get the Mage, camera, controls, combat and HUD.

## Collision

- All architecture, the blocks and the ground use mesh colliders taken from the visible mesh.
- Trees have a capsule collider on the trunk. Grass, moss, rubble and the background have none.
- Invisible walls are on the Ignore Raycast layer: they stop the character, but not the camera,
  the aim or the projectiles.
- The stair has 0.2 m steps, within the character controller's 0.3 m step height.

## Performance

Estimates from the generating code, not measurements:

- About 230 renderers and under 30,000 triangles in the whole scene.
- One directional light with shadows and one unshadowed point light. Nothing is baked.
- Twelve shared materials on one shader. The project's SRP Batcher is enabled.
- Vegetation is opaque geometry: no alpha testing and no transparency. Each grass blade is one
  triangle, and the grass outside the walls is merged into eight meshes.
- Distant objects cast no shadows. The Mobile pipeline asset limits shadows to 50 m.
- Environment objects are marked static.

Post-processing (bloom, tone mapping, vignette from the template's `SampleSceneProfile`) is
switched on for the camera. It has a cost on phones. To compare, untick **Post Processing** on
the `Main Camera`.

## Tests

Edit Mode tests in `Assets/Threefold/Tests/Editor/ProceduralMeshBuilderTests.cs` check that
every generated face points outwards (an inward face would be invisible), that cones have no
empty triangles, texture scale, sub-mesh assignment, and the shape of the ground. The same
orientation rule was also checked numerically outside Unity.

Not covered by automated tests: the asset generation, the scene assembly, the layout, and
everything about how the level looks and plays.

## What needs Unity validation

1. **Compilation.** In particular the two new assembly references for the render pipeline.
2. **Materials.** Generated from code against URP's Lit and procedural sky shaders.
3. **The look.** Scale, colour, fog, light direction, and whether the spire is framed by the arch.
4. **Playability.** Jump distances, the stair, the passage width, camera behaviour near walls.
5. **Performance on a phone.**

## Known limits

- No wind or animation in the vegetation; that needs a custom shader.
- No sound.
- Lighting is fully real-time. Baked lighting would look richer and cost less at runtime.
- The level is not a prefab or an additive scene; it lives in its own scene.

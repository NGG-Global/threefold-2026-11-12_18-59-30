# Visual Reference Prototype: the Reference Courtyard

A small courtyard for judging Threefold's visual direction, Soft Illustrated Realism
(`Docs/ArtBible.md`), against a reference picture supplied by the owner: a fantasy training courtyard
with ancient stone, vegetation, a distant castle and a third-person adventurer.

**Status (15 November 2026).** Implemented, assembled and tested in Unity on the build runner, rendered by
Unity in the Editor, and included in a development APK. **It does not match the reference picture, and
nobody should say it does.** The reference is a target for quality; the prototype is a first, deliberately
small step built from the game's existing primitive modules. What the renders show is described under
[What the renders show](#what-the-renders-show). Nothing here has been seen on a device. The look remains a
proposal under the art bible until the owner has reviewed it in the game and on a device.

The reference picture is treated as an artistic quality target. It is not a screenshot of the game, and
its architecture is not copied. It is not stored in the repository.

## What it is

One scene, `Assets/Scenes/ReferenceCourtyard.unity`, built by **Threefold > Setup > Reference Courtyard**
(and by the Android build) from the numbers in `ReferenceCourtyardLayout`. It reuses the Style Lab's
infrastructure throughout: the environment modules and materials (`EnvironmentAssets`), the lighting and
haze of the visual style's Golden Afternoon preset, the playable Mage rig with the game's own camera, and
the visual style applied by `VisualStyleSetup`.

| Asked for | What stands in the scene |
| --- | --- |
| A readable stone courtyard with hand-painted paving | A paved court 24 m by 26 m in the style's painted flagstones, with a kerb of uneven stones and a few worn slabs |
| One prominent ancient stone archway | The great arch: 8.2 m high, opening 5.2 m wide, a ring of thirteen stones, a ruined right-hand corner, and walls stepping down on both sides. It stands on the axis of the first view |
| Modular ruined walls and architectural details | The environment's wall, broken wall, low wall, column, broken column, fallen column and rubble modules: a standing wall with columns on the left, low ruins on the right |
| Selective vegetation, moss, grass and climbing vines | Seven vines (on the arch and the standing wall), eleven grass patches off the paving, six moss patches, seven trees. All opaque meshes |
| A distant mountain valley and castle silhouette | The courtyard stands on a terrace above a valley: four low wooded ridges, four bare ranges behind them, and a castle with a viaduct on a hill 240 m away |
| A clear focal point and third-person composition | The rune dais lies between the Mage and the arch; the first view is the game's own camera behind the Mage, and its composition is tested (below) |
| The Wayfarer character with the verified painted materials | The playable Mage; a test fails if he does not wear the Wayfarer mesh with the painted atlas and the style's material |
| Warm daylight and soft atmospheric depth | The style's Golden Afternoon preset: warm sun, cool ambient light, linear haze from 26 m to 360 m |

It is isolated from the game: no gameplay scene leads to it, it saves nothing, and it adds no rule. The
three training constructs and the objective are the playable rig's usual ones, so that the HUD is complete.

Nothing was added from outside the project, and no package or dependency was added.

Generated meshes are kept once made (`EnvironmentAssets.EnsureMesh`). A change of shape therefore needs a
new mesh name: raise `ReferenceCourtyardMeshes.Version` (now `v2`).

The scene uses the same post-processing switch as every level of the game (the existing global volume);

memory and heat have not been measured anywhere.

## Composition, as tested

`ReferenceCourtyardLayoutTests` check the first view as arithmetic on the layout, outside Unity: the arch
is whole and on the axis; the dais lies between the Mage and the arch; the castle is beside the arch,
above the horizon, inside the screen, and above every wall and tree of the layout that stands before it;
the left side is tall and the right side stays below the camera's eye; wooded ridges stay under the
castle. `ReferenceCourtyardAssemblyTests` check in Unity that the game's camera really has the values the
arithmetic assumes. These tests say that things are in view and unobstructed. They cannot say that the
view is attractive.

## What the renders show

`ReviewRenders` draws the scene from the layout's six viewpoints with Unity started from the command line
(workflow input **review_renders**, or `Tools\build\render-review.cmd`). The pictures are drawn by Unity's
renderer with the game's shader, materials and lighting through the scene's own camera, on the runner
(Direct3D 11, Intel integrated graphics). They are **Editor renders**: the Editor's quality level (`PC`)
and render-pipeline asset, not the phone's; nothing animated; no HUD or touch controls. They show
composition, materials and light. They do not show the game on a device.

What they show, plainly:

- **Works.** The composition is the one designed: arch on the axis with vines, castle and viaduct to the
  right on a wooded hill, ridges closing the valley, columns and trees framing the left, low ruins on the
  right. The painted stone and paving read as masonry. Warm light, cool shade and haze are present. The
  Mage wears the Wayfarer look with its contour line and faces the right way.
- **Far from the reference.** The forms are primitive: boxes, prisms and cones. Trees are stacked cones.
  The sky is a plain gradient without clouds. There is no water, no sculpted stone, no leaf detail, and the
  terrace is flat. Vines are flat leaves. The moss patches are bright flat shapes on the paving. The grass
  texture shows a chequered repeat on large areas. The castle is a silhouette and reads as one, but simply.
- **Not judged by these pictures.** The Mage stands in the model's rest pose, arms out: posing him outside
  Play mode had no effect, so animation, cloak and hair are unseen. Line weight in motion, texture
  shimmer, the HUD over the scene, and everything on a phone.

The first renders (run 21) are why the valley, the vines and the slabs were changed before this pull
request: single pyramid mountains closed the view, the vines read as loose leaves on wires, and the slabs
as loose plates.

![The arch in run 21, before the changes](VisualReferencePrototype/images/run21-2-arch.png)

One defect of the render tool is recorded, not explained: the first picture Unity drew after starting
showed one texture (the foliage's leaves) on every surface, and all later pictures were right. Two
pictures are now drawn and discarded first. Whether the game's own first frame can show the same has not
been checked.

## How to look at it

- **In a development APK:** open the diagnostics (three-finger tap), press **Scenes**, choose **Reference
  Courtyard**. If the buttons do not answer to touch, tap with four fingers while the diagnostics are shown
  to go to the next scene. See `Docs/CloudBuild.md`.
- **In the Editor:** Threefold > Setup > Reference Courtyard, then Play.
- **As pictures:** Actions > Android build > Run workflow, self-hosted, tick **review_renders**; download
  `threefold-review-renders-<number>`.

## Not verified

- Anything on a device: appearance, frame rate, memory, heat, touch.
- The scene in motion: animation, cloak and hair, line stability, texture shimmer.
- That the diagnostics' scene buttons answer to touch (the four-finger tap exists for that reason and is
  itself untried on a device).
- The art bible's acceptance test (section 10): it passes only after visual review and on-device
  performance verification, neither of which has happened.
